## The Capybara Democratization of Space

In the last weeks of August, there was a notable increase in news (and memes) about the presence of capybaras in Nordelta, Tigre, Buenos Aires Province. My curiosity as an urban planner led me to understand this phenomenon and especially why "the capybaras put the Wetlands Law on the agenda."

In a nutshell, more than 20 years ago, Nordelta was a wetland area — ecosystems with permanent or intermittent flooding, key for freshwater reserves and hosts of great biodiversity. Today it is an artificial habitat that emulates the natural one, made up of gated residential communities around artificial bodies of water.

The appearance of capybaras is due, among many reasons, to the displacement of their habitat by the growth of real estate developments, deforestation, fires, and recent changes in the urban dynamics of its inhabitants.

## Territorial Transformations in Nordelta

I focused on analyzing the territorial transformations that have occurred in Nordelta through the exploration and observation of a time-lapse of LANDSAT satellite images created with geemap (a Python package).

The time-lapse was built from band combinations that highlight changes in land use and water body transformations.

The most notable transformations occurred in 1999 and 2007. It can be observed that the urban fabric of these gated communities is determined by the morphology of the water bodies.

It is evident that the profitability of these developments is driven by the commercialization of scenic landscapes composed of water bodies. Initially, the water bodies had simple shapes. However, over the years, they began to grow more complex, forming channels and islands. More coastline meant more plots with waterfront access, especially along the canals.

If you look at the time-lapse, specifically the largest lake in Nordelta, you can see these transformations. In the 1990s it did not exist; by 2007 it had a "simple" morphology, and 14 years later it is complex with dimensions exceeding 180 hectares.

## Reflection

What satellite images cannot show is that these alterations to natural conditions are due to the filling of flood-prone areas and raising of ground levels above flood thresholds, which altered the pre-existing water dynamics.

Responsible and sustainable land use management is an aspect that rarely gets attention until environmental consequences emerge — such as flooding in vulnerable areas or territorial disputes between wildlife and residents. Not to mention the consequences of a sprawling development model on the periphery.

Urban planning is a matter of all scales, from municipalities approving real estate developments through environmental impact studies, to the national level with the Wetlands Law.
