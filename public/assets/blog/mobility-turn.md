Something that I had already studied and that many of us could infer is that Berlin has a great offer and coverage of an integrated public transport. However, I would like to share other concepts that have impressed me during my research and my stay here. Most of them are related to on-demand service-oriented mobility services. Here are my top 5 impressions about the mobility turn in Berlin.

## 1. Mobility Concepts (Mobilitätskonzept)

This instrument at different levels (such as districts or buildings) describes concrete measures to promote sustainable modes of transport. Imagine that promoters may reduce 1 parking space by providing 4 bicycle parking spots. This is suggested in those instruments. Additionally, it is possible to build districts that declare themselves "car-free." Therefore, they offer alternative modes to meet the mobility needs of their inhabitants.

## 2. Car-sharing

While this is not new for me, it impresses me how the trend is growing in Berlin. There are several companies providing services and you can find them all over the city. This model helps to reduce parking footprint, emissions and car ownership.

Something I was not aware of is the distinction between floating and station-based vehicle sharing. Free-floating vehicles can be parked anywhere within the service area of the company, while station-based vehicles must be picked up or parked at specific points.

![Car sharing in Berlin](/assets/blog/car-sharing.png)

## 3. Cargobikes (Lastenfahrräder)

This environmentally friendly alternative for daily trips seems really popular in Berlin. Cargo bikes are bicycles with a load-carrying design. It is common to see parents carrying their children to school or going for a ride on weekends. They are also used for logistics services.

Cargo bikes come in all shapes and sizes: free, rented, owned, 2 or 3-wheeled, electrically powered, or muscle-powered.

![Cargo bikes in Berlin](/assets/blog/cargo-bikes.png)

## 4. Mobility Hubs

These are places to stop, change and continue your journeys safely and quickly. Mobility hubs offer accessibility to public transport, parking spaces, shared mobility options (like scooters or e-bikes), and electric charging infrastructure, all in one place.

![Mobility hubs in Berlin](/assets/blog/mobility-hubs.jpg)

## 5. Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure

Car access is more sustainable than car ownership. One of the challenges for the shift to electric cars is the accessibility of charging. In Berlin, work is underway to increase this offer. Policies ensure that parking spaces in new buildings are provided with charging facilities, and charging points are appearing in public spaces.

![Electric vehicle charging in Berlin](/assets/blog/ecars.jpg)

My impressions in Berlin about micromobility, Mobility as a Service (MaaS), and electric car promotion have given me the clearest idea yet about how to begin the mobility turn. There are many ways to be mobile in a sustainable way, though of course, not everything is perfect, and there is still much to develop and integrate.
